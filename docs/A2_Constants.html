Appendix B — Physical Constants and Unit Conversions

The values below are rounded to six significant figures, which is more than enough for every calculation in this book. Exact values (marked with *) are fixed by the definition of the SI units. For the full list with all digits, see the NIST reference on constants (National Institute of Standards and Technology, n.d.).

Spectroscopists, chemists, and computational chemists all use different energy units. Table B.2 converts between the ones used in this book. To use it, read across a row: for example, \(1\;\text{eV} = 96.4853\;\text{kJ/mol}\).

A few more relations that are useful in practice:

  • Photon energy and wavelength: \(E=hc/\lambda\), with \(hc = 1239.84\;\text{eV nm}\). A photon of \(500\;\text{nm}\) therefore carries \(2.48\;\text{eV}\).
  • Wavenumbers: \(\tilde{\nu} = 1/\lambda\), so a wavelength of \(1\;\mu\text{m}\) corresponds to \(10{,}000\;\text{cm}^{-1}\).
  • Thermal energy at room temperature (\(298\;\text{K}\)): \(k_BT = 0.0257\;\text{eV} = 207\;\text{cm}^{-1}\), and \(RT = 2.48\;\text{kJ/mol}\).
  • Lengths: \(1\;\text{Å} = 10^{-10}\;\text{m} = 100\;\text{pm}\), and \(1\;a_0 = 0.529177\;\text{Å}\).
National Institute of Standards and Technology. n.d. The NIST Reference on Constants, Units, and Uncertainty. https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/.
